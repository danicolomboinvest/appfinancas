Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: competitive landscape after Mint, pricing, UA costs, bank connectivity, international expansion lessons, marketing compliance, launch sequencing.
Legend: "Verified" = backed by the cited source (often third-party, see caveats); "Inferred" = analyst reasoning or background knowledge NOT confirmed by a source in this session (listed under Inferences/Gaps only).

## Q1. Competitive landscape after Mint's shutdown (2024), pricing tiers, weaknesses, and gaps for a newcomer

### Takeaway
Mint's shutdown (announced late 2023, access ended 23 March 2024) pushed users to Credit Karma, which dropped budgets and custom categories. Monarch is the most-cited winner, though the evidence is mostly qualitative. The US paid tier now clusters around US$95–110/year (YNAB, Monarch, Copilot), with cheaper or variable options below it (Rocket Money, PocketGuard). That leaves room for a lower-priced, low-friction, niche-positioned (manual/AI-chat, multi-currency, Spanish/Portuguese) entrant.

### Cited Findings
**Mint transition (US)**
- Intuit announced in late 2023 that Mint would shut down and moved users to Credit Karma. Access ended on 23 March 2024. Per Monarch, Credit Karma supported only a subset of Mint's features and three years of history — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down-how-to-migrate-to-monarch-with-discount)
- Spending and net-worth tracking moved to Credit Karma, but monthly budgets and custom categories did not, which explains why budgeters left — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service?lv=true)
- On r/mintuit, the paid service Monarch "emerged as an early favorite" among alternatives — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2125596/the-popular-mint-budgeting-tool-is-shutting-down.html)
- Monarch reported a "massive influx" of users in the week after the announcement and slower bank syncing under the load (self-reported) — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down)
- Former Mint users also went to YNAB and Copilot Money (low-authority source, no figures) — [PocketClear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/what-happened-to-mint-2026.html)
- Monarch raised a US$75M Series B in May 2025 (FPV Ventures, Forerunner) at a reported US$850M post-money valuation, about US$94.8M raised in total (aggregator) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/monarch-money/)
- Copilot Money raised a US$6M Series A led by Adjacent (2024 article) — [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/?p=154626)
- I found no independent, quantified data on how Mint's users split between apps.

**US pricing benchmarks (third-party sources from 2026; sources conflict)**
- YNAB: US$109/year or US$14.99/month. 34-day trial, no card needed on web signup — [Unstar 2026 ranking](https://unstar.app/blog/ynab-rocket-money-monarch-everydollar-copilot-budget-apps-ranked-2026); [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/copilot-money-vs-ynab/)
- Monarch: US$99.99/year (Core). 7-day trial, card required. One source listed US$199.99, likely an error — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/ynab-vs-rocket-money-vs-monarch-budgeting-apps-ranked-2026); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/chatgpt-for-budgeting)
- Copilot Money: about US$95/year or US$13/month on Apple. Older listings say US$69.99/year — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/copilot-money-vs-ynab/)
- Rocket Money: free tier. Premium is "pay what you want," cited ranges are roughly US$4.99–12.99/month (sources vary) — [Resubs](https://resubs.app/resources/best-rocket-money-alternatives); [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/ynab-rocket-money-monarch-everydollar-copilot-budget-apps-ranked-2026)
- PocketGuard: about US$74.99/year, lifetime US$149.99 — [Appshunter 2026](https://appshunter.io/blog/best-budgeting-apps-iphone.md)

**UK pricing benchmarks**
- Emma (2026): Plus £4.99/month or £41.99/year, Pro £9.99/month or £83.99/year, Ultimate £14.99/month or £124.99/year. The free tier connects 2 bank accounts, Plus connects 4 — [NimbleFins](https://www.nimblefins.co.uk/Budgeting-apps-review-emma-YNAB-snoop). Which? lists Plus at £5.99/month (date unclear) — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/banking/banking-security-and-payment-methods/open-banking-budgeting-and-saving-apps-aLl3e0g9I7Ft)
- Snoop Plus: £5.99/month or £47.99/year. Trustpilot 3.6/5 from 478 reviews — [NimbleFins](https://www.nimblefins.co.uk/Budgeting-apps-review-emma-YNAB-snoop)
- Emma's own community forum has a thread on how costly Plaid-type aggregation is for apps — [Emma community](https://community.emma-app.com/t/plaid-is-expensive-how-do-you-survive/2051)

### Inferences
- Pricing anchor (inferred): the US "premium budgeting" band is US$95–110/year, and the UK band is about £40–125/year depending on tier. A newcomer can enter at about US$49–69/year (or a regional equivalent) and set itself apart through price, without the bank-sync cost (see Q3) that forces incumbents to charge about US$100.
- Weaknesses to exploit (inferred from the evidence above plus common review themes; not verified with scraped reviews in this session): (a) high price and steep learning curve (YNAB's method); (b) unreliable bank sync and broken connections (Monarch admitted slow syncing even in 2023); (c) mandatory card before the trial (Monarch); (d) iOS-only or Apple-centric products (Copilot is known as Apple-first, unverified here); (e) English-only interfaces and single-currency design; (f) privacy discomfort with handing bank credentials to aggregators.
- Positioning gaps a Brazilian team is well placed to fill (inferred): (1) **chat/voice-first logging** in a WhatsApp style, which fits Brazilian product DNA, where WhatsApp-bot finance assistants are common; (2) **multi-currency for immigrants** (Brazilian, Latino and Portuguese diasporas sending remittances and holding accounts in two countries); (3) **Spanish- and Portuguese-language** budgeting for US Hispanics, a group the main US apps serve poorly; (4) **couples/household** shared budgets; (5) **privacy-first / no bank linking** as a deliberate feature rather than a limitation.

### Gaps
- No systematic Reddit or App Store review mining was done in this session. A dedicated pass (r/ynab, r/MonarchMoney, r/personalfinance, App Store 1–2 star reviews) is needed to quote real complaints.
- EveryDollar pricing and user numbers were not confirmed (its premium tier was about US$79.99/year by recollection, unverified).
- No Rocket Money subscriber count was found. Rocket Money is owned by Rocket Companies, and its public filings could provide one.
- Canadian and Australian incumbents (e.g., Australia's Frollo/WeMoney, Canada's KOHO and others) were not researched.

## Q2. Download/revenue data by country; CPI vs ARPU by market

### Takeaway
Free, reliable country-level download and revenue data for budgeting apps was not available in this session (Sensor Tower, AppMagic and Appfigures are paywalled). The best public CPI signals are directional. Adjust reports that finance-app CPI fell in H1 2025, including North America dropping to about US$2.92. LatAm is the fastest-growing region for installs, but monetization there is weaker.

### Cited Findings
- Finance-app global CPI fell from US$1.51 to US$1.13 in H1 2025 (Adjust, via search summary) — [Adjust Finance App Insights 2025](https://www.adjust.com/blog/finance-app-insights-2025/)
- North American finance CPI fell from US$7.03 (2024) to US$2.92 (H1 2025) (Adjust, via search summary) — [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/finance-app-insights-2025/)
- LatAm led finance-app growth, with installs up 59% YoY in H1 2025 — [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/finance-app-insights-2025/)
- Liftoff 2022 (older data): LatAm users were cheapest to acquire (about US$1.60 CPI) but registered at only 18.3%. EMEA had the highest registration rate (34.8%). Cost per acquisition was EMEA US$93.02, APAC US$38.46 and North America US$26.15 — [Liftoff 2022 report](https://content.liftoff.io/hubfs/2022/Reports/2022%20Mobile%20Finance%20Apps%20Report/2022%20Finance%20Apps%20Report%20-%20EN.pdf); [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/mobile/new-liftoff-data-reveals-customers-flock-to-fintech-as-economy-tightens-offers-finance-app-marketers-guidance-on-user-acquisition/)
- Meta-ads finance CPI (SuperAds, Oct 2024–Oct 2025, "directional"): global average about 11.85–12.67 (currency presumably USD) with a large spread. Germany averaged about 9.41 and settled near 6 in early 2025. The UK and Canada had no finance data points — [SuperAds Germany](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/finance/germany); [SuperAds Norway](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/finance/norway); [SuperAds UK](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/finance/united-kingdom)
- Note the conflict: Adjust's CPIs (about US$1–3) cover all finance apps and all channels, including cheap Android/LatAm traffic. SuperAds' Meta-only figures (about US$6–12) are far higher. A subscription budgeting app targeting iOS in Tier-1 markets should plan with the higher range.
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (75k apps, over US$10B revenue): nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in the first month. Cheaper annual plans retain up to 36% after one year, compared with 6.7% for high-priced monthly plans. Median revenue per install at 60 days is US$0.31 overall (US$0.63 for AI apps) — [RevenueCat launch post](https://revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch); [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)
- RevenueCat's 2026 report covers more than 115k apps and over US$16B in revenue, and defines D35 download-to-paid conversion. The category and region breakdowns sit in the gated PDF — [RevenueCat 2026 report](https://revenuecat.com/report)

### Inferences
- Revenue per install is the decision metric (inferred): with a median RPI of about US$0.31 at 60 days, a Tier-1 iOS CPI of US$3–10 means paid UA only pays back if the app sits well above median conversion, or if LTV is built on annual plans. Lead with annual plans, discounted and highlighted on the paywall, since RevenueCat shows they retain best.
- Market attractiveness (inferred from general app-economy knowledge, NOT verified here): Canada, Australia and the UK typically show CPIs below the US with ARPU near US levels, and English localization costs nothing extra. These are the classic soft-launch markets. Portugal offers cheap CPI and Portuguese language, but low ARPU and a small population; it suits product validation and the diaspora, not revenue. Mexico, Colombia and Spanish-speaking LatAm offer cheap installs but low paid conversion (consistent with Liftoff's 18.3% registration and the LatAm volume growth).
- US Hispanics (inferred): pay at US price levels while possibly reachable with Spanish creatives at lower competition. This needs testing.

- No Sensor Tower, AppMagic or Appfigures country rankings or revenue estimates for budgeting apps from 2024–2026 were retrieved (paywalled). Recommend a trial or one-off purchase of an Appfigures/AppMagic report for "Finance > budgeting" by country.
- No finance-specific RevenueCat conversion or price medians (gated PDF). Download the 2026 PDF.

## Q3. Bank aggregation options by market, costs, and whether manual-entry apps can succeed

### Takeaway
Bank sync is a real cost and reliability burden: Plaid in the US is roughly US$1–2 per connection plus monthly fees per item, with minimums, and Europe's free option (GoCardless/Nordigen) has reportedly closed to new signups. A manual-first or chat-first app can launch in every market without aggregator contracts and add sync later where the economics work.

### Cited Findings
- Plaid publishes no universal list price. Pricing can be a one-time fee per connection, a monthly fee per active connection, a fee per request, or a fee per data volume. US plans are Pay as You Go, Growth and Custom. Transaction-type products are billed as subscriptions while an access token exists, so removing the Item ends the billing — [Plaid billing docs](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing)
- Third-party estimates put Plaid at about US$1.50–2.00 per link at low volume — [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/api-management/plaid/)
- One developer reported a Plaid quote of US$1.25 per initial institution connection plus US$0.25/month per institution, with a US$500 monthly minimum — [Founder Archive](https://founderarchive.beehiiv.com/p/inside-plaids-pricing-strategy); see also [Emma community thread on Plaid cost](https://community.emma-app.com/t/plaid-is-expensive-how-do-you-survive/2051)
- GoCardless Bank Account Data (formerly Nordigen, the free EU/UK open-banking AIS API) reportedly shows a "new signups disabled" page; existing users continue. This is unofficial and unconfirmed by GoCardless — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/johnfrandsen/gocardless-bank-account-data-alternatives-what-to-use-when-signups-are-disabled-326d); the free tier was previously documented for commercial and personal use — [Firefly III docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/how-to/data-importer/import/gocardless/); a 2026 guide says the free tier is limited and increasingly bundled with payments — [DEV Community 2026](https://dev.to/johnfrandsen/nordigen-alternatives-in-2026-a-developers-guide-to-european-bank-data-apis-3pie)
- The Nordigen acquisition by GoCardless — [Finance Director Europe](https://www.financedirectoreurope.com/news/gocardless-nordigen-open-banking-data-provider-acquisition/)
- In LatAm, Mobills was building a bank-integration partnership with Belvo (2021). Feasibility of local bank integration shaped which countries it chose (Mexico and Colombia) — [Finsiders Brasil](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/reportagem-exclusiva-fintechs/mobills-prepara-expansao-latam-para-segundo-semestre)
- UK open-banking budgeting apps (Emma, Snoop) are mainstream enough that Which? publishes consumer guides on them — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/banking/banking-security-and-payment-methods/open-banking-budgeting-and-saving-apps-aLl3e0g9I7Ft)

### Inferences
- Aggregator map (from background knowledge, NOT verified this session; confirm with each vendor): US/Canada: Plaid, MX, Finicity (Mastercard), Akoya; UK/EU: TrueLayer, Tink (Visa), Yapily, GoCardless BAD, Salt Edge; Australia: Basiq and Frollo under CDR (Consumer Data Right), plus Plaid; Brazil: Pluggy, Belvo, Open Finance Brasil; Mexico/Colombia: Belvo. Most enterprise aggregators quote privately and set monthly minimums (often in the hundreds to thousands of US$), which is hard to justify before product–market fit.
- Unit economics (inferred): at US$0.25–0.50/month per linked institution and 2–4 institutions per user, sync can cost US$0.50–2/month per paying user, which is 10–40% of a US$5–8/month price. This is why sync-based apps price at about US$100/year. A manual-first app has close to zero marginal data cost.
- Manual-entry viability (inferred): there are successful manual or semi-manual apps (YNAB originally ran on manual entry; Brazil's own Mobills and Organizze grew largely on manual entry before Open Finance). The risk is friction and churn. Mitigations: AI chat or voice logging, receipt or screenshot OCR, SMS or email parsing, CSV import, and Apple Wallet/FinanceKit on iOS (US only, unverified). Market the lack of sync as "privacy, no bank password," which addresses the reliability complaints users have about sync apps.
- Recommendation (inferred): launch manual/AI-first everywhere; add CSV/OFX import early; integrate an aggregator only in the first market that proves willingness to pay (likely UK via TrueLayer/Tink, or US via Plaid Pay-as-You-Go), and offer sync as a premium-tier feature to cover its cost.

### Gaps
- No official TrueLayer, Tink, MX, Finicity, Belvo or Pluggy price lists were retrieved (most do not publish them). Contact sales.
- GoCardless signup status is unconfirmed from an official source.
- No hard data on retention of manual vs bank-synced budgeting apps.

## Q4. Lessons from Brazilian/LatAm fintechs abroad and non-US apps that won in the US

### Takeaway
The clearest model is Cleo: a UK-founded AI-chat finance app that refocused on the US and left the UK and Canada. It reached about US$150M ARR by late 2024 (reported) and is considering returning to the UK. Brazilian budgeting apps (Mobills) found organic demand in Mexico, Colombia and Argentina but announced country-by-country LatAm expansion plans (2021) for which no evidence of large-scale success was found.

### Cited Findings
- Cleo was founded in London in 2016, expanded to the US around 2019, and dropped UK and Canada operations during the pandemic to focus on the US. Sources differ on the timing — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/note/cleo-s-rise-in-the-u-s); [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/ai-fintech-cleo-return-uk)
- Cleo turnover was US$65.9M in 2023, with adjusted break-even by December 2023 — [UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/fintech/cleo-revenue-financials-20240704)
- Cleo was reported at about US$150M ARR and more than 7M users (November 2024), and is weighing a return to the UK — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/ai-fintech-cleo-return-uk). Mid-2025 ARR estimates of about US$280M (aggregator citing Sacra; lower confidence) — [Neuronfeed](https://neuronfeed.com/startups/cleo-ai); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/cleo)
- Cleo raised a US$44M Series B led by EQT Ventures (2020) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/10/cleo-series-b). Aggregators list a US$38.8M convertible note in March 2025 at a reported US$1B valuation — [Multiples](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/cleo)
- Mobills (Fortaleza): downloaded in more than 138 countries, with about 20% of users from abroad, mostly Mexico, Colombia and Argentina. It planned to enter Mexico and Colombia first (already had paying subscribers there), then the US and Europe in 2022, "country by country" (2021 statements) — [MobileTime](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/21/01/2021/mobills-aposta-em-expansao-internacional-para-2021/); [Finsiders Brasil](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/reportagem-exclusiva-fintechs/mobills-prepara-expansao-latam-para-segundo-semestre)

### Inferences
- Lesson 1, follow organic demand (inferred from Mobills): check the current app's analytics for organic installs and payers by country before choosing markets. Spanish-speaking LatAm and the diaspora probably already show some.
- Lesson 2, focus beats breadth (inferred from Cleo): Cleo won by concentrating on one large market (the US) with a distinct voice (a playful, "roast me" AI chat persona aimed at younger users) and left smaller markets. A Brazilian team with a limited budget should pick one revenue market and treat the others as test or diaspora markets.
- Lesson 3, AI chat is a proven US wedge (inferred): Cleo's success shows US consumers will engage with a conversational finance assistant. A WhatsApp-style or voice entry flow is a credible differentiator, not a novelty.
- Lesson 4 (inferred, unverified): Nubank's moves into Mexico and Colombia were banking/credit plays requiring licences, and are not directly comparable to a budgeting app, but show that Brazilian brands can win in Spanish-speaking LatAm with localized teams. Fintonic (Spain) and Emma (UK) are examples of strong home-market budgeting apps that did not become US leaders, which suggests the US requires dedicated investment.
- Diaspora angle (inferred): Brazilians abroad (concentrated in the US — Massachusetts, Florida, New Jersey — plus Portugal, Japan and the UK) can be reached via Portuguese-language creatives and community channels at low CPMs, and need two-currency budgeting (BRL + local) and remittance tracking.

### Gaps
- No sourced information this session on Organizze or Olivia AI internationally, Nubank Mexico/Colombia metrics, Fintonic, or Emma's US launch outcome.
- No confirmation of whether Mobills actually launched in Mexico/Colombia/US after 2021 or how it performed.

## Q5. Compliance for marketing finance apps abroad

### Takeaway
In the US, the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated on 8 July 2025, but ROSCA, the old negative-option rule and state auto-renewal laws still apply. The FTC rule banning fake reviews and testimonials has been in effect since 21 October 2024, with penalties up to US$51,744 per violation. Meta requires the "Financial products and services" special ad category for US-targeted finance ads (since 14 January 2025), which limits targeting. In the UK, FCA financial-promotion rules cover regulated products. A pure budgeting tool with no credit or investment features is likely outside them, but finfluencer content must avoid touching regulated products.

### Cited Findings
**US – subscriptions and cancellation**
- On 8 July 2025 the Eighth Circuit (Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC, No. 24-3137) vacated the entire Click-to-Cancel rule shortly before its 14 July 2025 effective date, on procedural grounds (no preliminary regulatory analysis), not on the merits — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Gunderson](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)
- The 1973 Negative Option Rule and ROSCA remain in force and the FTC continues to enforce ROSCA. State automatic-renewal laws also still apply — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/node/15872); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1653178/with-click-to-cancel-rule-now-vacated-by-8th-circuit-whats-next-for-ftc)

**US – reviews, testimonials, endorsements**
- The FTC's Trade Regulation Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials took effect on 21 October 2024. It bans fake reviews (including AI-generated ones) and incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment, limits insider reviews, and bars presenting company-controlled review sites as independent. Civil penalties reach US$51,744 per knowing violation — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20241009-ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-testimonials-and-touts-new-rule-at-open-commission-meeting); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-testimonials)

**Ad platforms**
- Meta: since 14 January 2025, advertisers in the US or targeting the US must use the "Financial products and services" special ad category for finance campaigns. It replaces the CREDIT input and restricts targeting: per a third-party summary, no lookalikes, saved audiences or detailed targeting tied to protected classes, and no location exclusion — [Meta Marketing API docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category); [Data Axle](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/)
- Meta's credit-ad policy still applies in the US, Canada and parts of Europe — [Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)

**UK – FCA**
- Promotions of financial products by unauthorised persons on social media can breach the financial promotion restriction in s.21 FSMA 2000 (a criminal offence). Promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading and "standalone compliant." The FCA consulted on finfluencer guidance (GC23/2), and the Consumer Duty raises the bar — [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/en-ie/news-and-insights/insights/2023/8/finfluencers-and-the-fca-a-review-of-the-proposed-financial-promotions-on-social-media-guidance)

### Inferences
- Whether Meta's special ad category applies (inferred): a budgeting app may or may not fall under "financial products and services." Meta's definition centres on credit, insurance, investments, banking, etc. Expect Meta's classifier to flag it and plan creatives and targeting to work under the broad-targeting constraints (Advantage+ / broad audiences, creative-led targeting), which is how Meta campaigns are typically run now anyway.
- US subscription compliance checklist (inferred from ROSCA and state laws such as California's ARL; verify with counsel): disclose price, renewal terms and trial-to-paid conversion clearly before purchase; get express consent; send renewal and trial-ending reminders; provide simple online cancellation. Apple and Google handle billing and cancellation for in-app purchases, which lowers but does not remove risk, especially for web checkout.
- Influencers (inferred; FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255, revised 2023, not fetched this session): require clear "#ad" / "paid partnership" disclosures in-video and in-caption. Avoid income or savings claims ("save $500/month") unless substantiated as typical. The same applies in the UK (ASA/CAP code) and the EU.
- UK (inferred): a budgeting app with no credit, investment or savings products is generally not making a "financial promotion" under FSMA. Adding credit-score features, loans, cashback-investing or affiliate links to regulated products would trigger it, as would influencers recommending specific financial products in app content. Open-banking access in the UK/EU requires an FCA-authorised AISP or use of an aggregator's licence ("agent" model).
- Privacy (inferred, not sourced this session): GDPR (EU/UK, including Portugal) requires a lawful basis, DPAs with processors, an EU/UK representative if the company has no establishment there (GDPR art. 27), and international-transfer mechanisms (SCCs) for data held in Brazil or the US. CCPA/CPRA applies to US businesses above revenue or data thresholds, and the "Do not sell/share" rule matters for ad SDKs and pixels. Brazil's LGPD compliance is a good base. Apple ATT and Google's privacy changes limit attribution, so SKAN/AdAttributionKit and MMP setup are needed.
- App stores (inferred): Apple and Google require subscriptions via in-app purchase for digital features. Since 2025 US court rulings, Apple allows external purchase links in the US (unverified detail; check current Apple guidelines). Apple guideline 5.1.1(ix) restricts certain financial apps to legal entities (verify). Web-to-app checkout can improve margins in the US.

### Gaps
- TikTok's financial-services ad policy was not retrieved. Check TikTok's Ads Policy "Financial services" page per country.
- No official FTC page fetched for the Endorsement Guides, and no confirmation of whether the FTC re-proposed the click-to-cancel rule in 2026.
- No confirmation of the final FCA finfluencer guidance (FG24/1, believed to be 2024, unverified).
- No sources this session for GDPR/CCPA specifics, Canada (PIPEDA/Quebec Law 25), Australia (Privacy Act/CDR) or Apple/Google subscription rule text.

## Q6. Recommended launch sequencing, soft-launch markets, test budgets, first-year UA benchmarks

### Takeaway
No sourced benchmark for "typical first-year paid UA budget" was found. Sequencing below is inferred from the CPI/ARPU signals above and standard subscription-app practice: validate in cheap English- and Portuguese/Spanish-speaking markets, then scale in the US. Plan around annual plans, given RevenueCat's retention data and a median 60-day RPI of about US$0.31.

### Cited Findings
- Annual plans retain much better than monthly (36% vs 6.7% after a year for cheap annual vs expensive monthly), but about 30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in month one (often trial or auto-renew turned off) — [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)
